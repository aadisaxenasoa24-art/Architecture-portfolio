# Antilia, Altamount Road, Mumbai — research dossier

Compiled 1 October 2026 for a B.Arch critical case study. Five research passes, each checked against the source pages where they could be opened.

**How to use it.** Every claim carries its source (publisher, date, URL) and a confidence rating. Quotes are verbatim and 40 words or fewer. Lines marked ANALYSIS or "my calculation" are derived figures, not sourced facts. Contradictions between sources are listed, not resolved by guessing. Where a source could not be opened, the dossier says so.

**Headline corrections to common claims**
- Height: 173 m is the CTBUH figure (measured from the lowest entrance). The structural engineer gives 160 m above ground level with the helipad at +159 m. "60 storeys" is a myth; 27 floors, many double or triple height.
- Area: about 42,000 m² gross (engineer); 400,000 sq ft (37,161 m²) is the press figure. BMC counted only 4,778 m² for residential FSI (2007).
- "Survives a magnitude-8 earthquake" is unsourced; the engineer adopted Zone IV design parameters for a Zone III city.
- The land was sold by an orphanage trust in 2002 (permission 27 Aug, deed 21 Nov, Rs 21.05 crore). Its Waqf status was contested for two decades; the Bombay HC dismissed the PIL on 28 Jul 2025. Do not call the purchase "illegal" as fact.
- Antilia is not the tallest building near it: Lodha Altamount (195 m) stands 91 m east; The Imperial towers (210 m roof, 256 m to spire) and Iora (195 m) are within about 500 m.

## 0. How to read this sheet

**Confidence**

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | A primary source (designer, engineer, database, the family on record), or two or more independent reputable outlets that agree. |
| **Medium** | One reputable outlet, or several outlets that all trace back to a single unnamed source. |
| **Low** | Repeated but unsourced, attributed only to "reports", or found only on weak sites. Treat as rumour. |

**Primary vs secondary.** The best primary sources are:
- **S3**: the structural engineer's own newsletter (Sterling Engineering, July 2010).
- **S1**: Perkins+Will's design principal, interviewed in Architectural Record (2007).
- **S4**: the CTBUH Skyscraper Center database.
- **S6**: a BMC official quoted on the approved plan (Indian Express, 2007).
- **S10, S11, S12**: Nita Ambani's on-record interview (Vanity Fair, 2012, relayed by BBC and PTI).

Everything else is journalism, and much of it is copied from the same few early reports.

**Access notes. Read these before reusing a quote.**
- nytimes.com blocks automated access. Article metadata (title, author, date, summary) came from NYT's own oEmbed endpoint. The 2010 text came from a verbatim reproduction posted on 1 Nov 2010 (sikhphilosophy.net thread 33023). Re-check quotes against the NYT original or a library database before you print them.
- dnaindia.com blocks direct download. DNA text came through a fetch tool that was asked for exact sentences. Treat DNA quotes as near-certain, not guaranteed.
- I could not open the following: Forbes 2010 and 2014, the Washington Post (2011), SCMP (2020), India Today (2017), Trendhunter, and the Wayback Machine. Where Forbes 2014 matters, I use the PTI relay of it (S19).
- The web-search quota ran out partway through. Later lookups used direct fetches of known URLs. Some gaps listed in §11 might close with more searching.

**What the firms themselves said**
- **Perkins+Will (2007).** Ralph Johnson: "There's been a lot of crazy things floating around… but there's actually a lot of positive things to talk about because it's an interesting building." (S1)
- **Perkins+Will (2007), on the green walls.** Johnson: "You can use the whole wall almost like a tree and increase the green area of the site by five or 10 times over what it would be if you just did a green roof." (S1)
- **Perkins+Will website, as quoted by Indian Express (June 2007).**
  - The design was "in direct response to unique programmatic elements and a desire to maximise the views around the site".
  - The frame is an "undulating ribbon, or snake frame".
  - "The vertical surfaces of the frame will be articulated with a steel lattice and contain a series of planter beds, effectively transforming the facades into living green walls which will filter light and enhance the local micro-climate of each level." (S6)
- **Perkins+Will's site today.** A search of perkinswill.com for "antilia" returns **0 results** (S30). The firm does not currently publish the project.
- **Silence in 2012.** "Both the tower's architect, Chicago-based Perkins & Will, and its interior designer, Atlanta-based Hirsch Bender Associates, decline to comment on the situation." (S2)
- **HBA (2008).** The house would be "a 27-story skyscraper in downtown Mumbai with a cost nearing $2 billion, says Thomas Johnson, director of marketing at Hirsch Bedner Associates." (S5)
- **The designers, via Forbes (2008):**
  - They were "altering floor plans, design elements and concepts as the building is constructed".
  - "no two floors are alike in either plans or materials used".
  - "85% of the materials and labor will come from outside the U.S., most of it from India." (S5)
- **Sterling (2010), on the architect's reaction.** Perkins+Will "were pleased to see that their original architectural intent was kept intact even after the conversion" from steel to RCC. (S3)
- **Confidentiality.** Ambani "required his designers, decorators and other contractors to sign confidentiality agreements" (NYT 2010, S8). This explains why few primary documents exist.
- **Leighton.** No public statement, scope or contract value was found (S29).

## 7. Design concept, composition and structure

### 7.1 Concept (in the designers' own words, or close to them)
- **A green band that sorts the programme.** Architectural Record: "a band of vertical and horizontal gardens that demarcates the tower's different program elements". The garden level "will separate the ground-floor parking and conference center from residential space above". Trellises carry "panels that contain hydroponically grown plants". (S1)
- **Gardens for climate.** The vertical gardens "will help shade the building and reduce the urban heat island effect" (S1). Forbes: "The plants act as an energy-saving device by absorbing sunlight" (S5).
- **Snake frame and steel lattice.** From the Perkins+Will website (via S6): an "undulating ribbon, or snake frame", with façades turned into "living green walls" by a "steel lattice" holding "planter beds". The full 40-word quote is in §3.
- **Vaastu.** Architectural Record: "the overall plan is based on the square, which is Vaastu's basic geometric unit, and a garden level occupies the tower's midsection, the point where all energies converge according to the Vaastu Purusha Mandala." (S1) Forbes: "Antilla's shape is based on Vaastu" (S5).
- **The garden void and its columns.** Forbes: "In profile, the rebar-enforced beams form a "W" shape that supports the upper two-thirds of the building while creating an open-air atrium of gardens, flowers and lawns." (S5) The engineer describes the same thing as the "inverted pyramid" of inclined columns (S3; see §7.3).
- **Living at the top.** Nita Ambani: "We made our home right at the top because we wanted the sunlight… so it's an elevated house on top of a garden" (S10).
- **Interior motifs and materials.** "The design, she says, is based on the lotus and the sun, and is decorated using rare wood, marble, mother-of-pearl and crystal, crafted by Indian artisans." (S10; also S12) Forbes adds silver-covered railings and ginkgo-leaf sinks (S5).
- **No repetition.** "no two floors are alike in either plans or materials used" (S5).
- **Exterior.** NYT: "a cantilevered sheath of steel and glass" (S8).

### 7.2 Composition (my visual reading of the Commons photos in §9; not a sourced claim)
- A stack of horizontal 'trays' (deep slab edges), offset from one another and projecting as cantilevers.
- Slender round columns between some of the trays.
- Vertical fins and planted screen panels on the slab edges.
- A tall open garden zone in the lower third, carried on inclined V-shaped columns.
- A thin canopy roof over the top floor.
- Use this as a description of the photographs, not as design intent.

### 7.3 Structural system (primary source S3 unless noted)
- **Steel scheme abandoned.** The original steel frame by Arup UK was dropped. The members were "very heavy and almost impossible to fabricate, transport through the city to the site and to erect", and site butt-welding was "impractical and unreliable". "Hence after careful review, it was decided to convert the design into an RCC framed structure."
- **Same member sizes.** "There was no significant increase in sizes of the structural members." Instead, columns were "heavily reinforced in order to retain the original cross-section".
- **CTBUH classification:** "Concrete-Steel Composite" (S4). Visible steel elements include the platform for the ballroom chandeliers and the helical stairs (S3).
- **Foundation.** "3 meter thick RCC raft foundation designed by M/s. Arup", on "very hard rock" at lower-basement level.
- **Lateral system.** "the elevator and stair cores form the main spine of the building. However, the service core is eccentrically placed." Other areas sit on column grids of "about 7 meters x 5 meters".
- **Column-free ballroom and parking.** Under the tower, the "main columns were pushed back towards the core". This also gave "column free car parking floors above the banquet hall".
- **Garden-level split.** "Above the main garden at 44 meters level, each "mega column" was split into 4 tapering circular columns forming an inverted pyramid." These are 1,600 mm in diameter and stand 22 m free. They were cast in purpose-made two-piece semicircular steel forms on staging, which stayed until the floor above was cast and cured. S3 names no separate transfer girders for Antilia; transfer girders are mentioned only for other projects in the same newsletter.
- **Cantilevers.** The floors at 101 m and 108 m are "cantilevered by about 9 meters beyond the front row of columns". They were built on about 56 m of braced staging that stayed in place for "over two months".
- **Pre-tilted core.** The analysis predicted a westward tilt under dead load, because the heavily stressed columns shorten more than the core walls. "The service core was therefore, constructed with a carefully calculated "tilt" towards the east so that, under fully loaded condition, it would come back to a nearly vertical position."
- **Analysis.** A space-frame model in STAAD-Pro, using seismic Zone IV parameters, with the response reduction factor taken as 3 and then reduced to 2, "to rely on strength than on ductility".
- **Wind.** BMT tests set the target acceleration at 8–10 milli-g.

### 7.4 Orientation
- **Entrance:** "grand entrance canopy on the eastern side" (S3).
- **Service core:** "located on the eastern face of the building" (S3).
- **Views:** the residential floors "enjoy panoramic views of the city in all directions" (S3). Forbes: the top floors "offer panoramic views of the Arabian Sea" (S5).
- **Vastu critique:** reports say the **east side** lacks openings for morning light (S2, S31).
- *My inference, not stated by any source:* an east-facing solid service core fits with a less open east façade. This is worth testing on plans, but do not present it as fact.

## 10. Contradictions

1. **Height.** 173 m (CTBUH, The Age) vs 160 m above ground with the helipad at 159 m (Sterling) vs 172 m approved (BMC) vs 550 ft (Forbes 2008) vs 570 ft (VF, BBC, AFP) vs 490 ft (2007 design). See the reconciliation hypothesis in §4.1.
2. **Floor count.** 27 vs 24 (Perkins+Will in Oct 2007, although Indian Express already said "27-storey" in June 2007). The equivalent height is "40 storeys" (BMC, VF/BBC) vs "60" (rumour per S1; Wikipedia via Trendhunter).
3. **Area.**
   - Totals: 400,000 sq ft (≈37,161 m²) vs about 42,000 m² (engineer).
   - Partial figures: 60,000 sq ft of residential space (NYT, anonymous) and 35,000 sq ft for the family (2007).
   - The Age wrongly says the top four floors take up "about 37,000 square metres".
   - The BMC-approved residential figure is only 4,778.09 m² (permissible 4,939.81 m²). This is a different measure: FSI area, not gross floor area.
4. **Cost.** Every figure is an estimate: "nearing $2 billion" (HBA), $1 billion, "a figure disputed by people familiar with the project" (NYT), "$77 million" to build (The Age), $1–2 billion (Forbes 2014), Rs 13,000 crore (HT 2017), "$4.6 billion" (FE 2023, no basis given).
5. **Construction start.** 2006 (CTBUH) vs "commenced, in 2008" (VF). VF's date is contradicted by Architectural Record, which reported that construction had **reached the mid-section garden by mid-2007**. Other spans also disagree: "seven years of construction" (The Age), "a four-year process" (Forbes).
6. **Contractor.** Leighton held the original contract (CTBUH, SMH) but had left by August 2008 (SMH). The claim that it was "completed by B. E. Billimoria" (Wikipedia) has no source.
7. **Perkins+Will office:** Chicago (S1, S3) vs Dallas (S5). **HBA office:** Los Angeles (S5) vs Atlanta (S2, which also misspells the firm "Hirsch Bender").
8. **Residential floors:** 4 (engineer, The Age) vs "top six floors" (Wikipedia, AD India).
9. **Helipads:** three on the roof (NYT and others) vs "a helipad at 159 meters" (engineer) vs one proposed and not cleared (BMC, 2007). They have been reported as non-operational since 2010.
10. **Parking:** 168 vs 160 cars. "Underground" (Forbes 2014 via PTI, HuffPost) vs above the upper-basement ballroom (engineer).
11. **Elevators:** 9 (engineer and four outlets) vs 10 (Wikipedia infobox, unsourced).
12. **Move-in.** "In the one month since Mukesh Ambani moved in" (TOI, Nov 2010) vs not yet moved in (NYT, Oct 2011) vs September 2011 (Nita Ambani via VF/BBC; DNA) vs "shifted… in 2012" (FE 2023).
13. **Housewarming:** "October 28" (The Age) vs "Nov. 28" (NYT); an open house took place on 26 Nov 2010 (AFP).

## A. Land and waqf controversy

### A-1. Pre-history of the plot: an 1890 height covenant and a 1957 acquisition
- **Claim:**
  - In May 1890, Dady Manekji Limji conveyed the 38,000 sq yd "Altamount" property to Currimji Alibhai.
  - He kept the 25,821 sq yd "New Bunce" or "Mount Petit" estate. That estate was burdened by a covenant: no structures above the height of Altamount's compound wall, which was "7 feet from the level of existing public road known as Altamount Road".
  - New Bunce later passed to Jivajirao Scindia, Maharaja of Gwalior (indenture of 24 Dec 1948). It was subdivided around 2 Apr 1957.
  - "The Maharaja of Gwalior conveyed Plot No.6 to Currimbhoy Ebrahim Khoja Orphanage Trust".
  - The 1957 deed of mutual covenant kept the 7-ft restriction for Plots 3, 4 and 5.
  - "remaining part of plot No.6 has been acquired by BEST."
  - The topography falls away from the orphanage plot: "plot Nos.3, 4, 5 and 6 are in descending level with the plot No.6 at the highest point and the plot No.3 at the lowest level."
- **Source:** Bombay HC, *The Pashmina Co-op. Housing Society Ltd. v. Latif M. Hassambhoy & Ors.*, First Appeal No. 1128 of 1995, judgment of 9 Jan 2024 (Anuja Prabhudessai J.), 2024:BHC-AS:953. https://indiankanoon.org/doc/41007630/ (opened)
- **Corroboration:** the Commission of Inquiry report (§A-3) records: "Trust was registered under B.P.T.Act on 5-11-1952. The property in question was acquired on 2-4-1957 (P.3920)."
- **Confidence:** High that the judgment and the report say this. Medium-High that Plot 6 is the plot later sold to ACPL: the 1957 dates match, and ACPL was impleaded as Respondent 12.
- **Notes:**
  - The judgment gives the 1890 indenture as both 22/05/1890 and 23/05/1890.
  - Popular accounts say Currimbhoy Ebrahim "donated" this land in 1895. These records instead show the trust acquiring this plot in 1957, long after the founder's era.
  - The trust itself is older. Its trust deed is dated 27 Jun 1896 per the Commission report. Other sources give 1890 (JPC 2008), 1892 (Caravan 2016) and 1894 (The National 2011).
  - Treat "land donated in 1895" as unsupported for *this* plot.

### A-2. Neighbour litigation over the height covenant (1981–2024)
- **Claim:**
  - In S.C. Suit No. 3252 of 1981, the owner of the road plot (6A) and the orphanage trustees sued Pashmina CHS (Plot 3) to enforce the 7-ft covenant.
  - On 14 Sep 1995 the City Civil Court ordered demolition of construction above 7 ft.
  - On 9 Jan 2024 the Bombay HC allowed Pashmina's appeal: "Hence, the restriction is deemed obsolete as a result of changes in the character of the property or the neighbourhood."
  - The HC noted that the trustees had allowed buildings above 7 ft on Plots 4 and 5, and that "several high rise buildings have come in the vicinity".
- **Source:** same judgment, paras 1–5 and 27–33 (opened)
- **Confidence:** High
- **Note:** Useful urban-morphology evidence for the case study. A Victorian-era low-rise covenant on Cumballa Hill eroded into high-rise redevelopment. The judgment does not discuss Antilia itself, and records nothing about ACPL's stance in the appeal.

### B-2. FSI: what can and cannot be said
- **Base rule.** "In Mumbai the permitted FSI is uniform and in 1991 was fixed at 1.33 for the island city and 1.00 for the suburbs".

- **Exemptions under DCR 1991.** Under the sanctioned DCR 1991 computation, areas such as parking floors, staircase/lift/lobby areas and "free of FSI" features (flower beds, voids, meter rooms, etc.) sat outside the FSI count.
  - Source: MCHI presentation, *Modified D.C. Regulation (Mumbai)* (undated). It contrasts the "sanctioned D.C.Reg.1991" computation with a proposed modification that adds 35% "Compensatory FSI". https://mchi.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/cafa760b5e79af0f36358f66715632f7.pdf (opened)
  - Confidence: Medium. It is a general summary, not specific to Antilia.
- **Arithmetic (my calculation, not a sourced fact):**

  - The gap would have to be explained by FSI-exempt areas (parking, refuge, service and plant floors, lift/stair cores, double-height volumes), and possibly other concessions.

### 4.4 What this means for a tall glazed tower (my analysis, not a sourced claim)
- **Wind exposure.** The ridge-top site (~50 m) plus 173 m of height puts the roof at about 220 m above MSL, facing an open sea fetch to the W and NW. A 44 m/s basic wind speed, with the terrain-category and height factors that apply above the surrounding mid-rise fabric, governs cladding and façade pressures. Monsoon winds (W/SW, 70–75% of days) drive rain horizontally, which tests joints, sills and the edges of cantilevered terraces.
- **Solar and thermal load.** At 19° N the sun is high and nearly overhead in May–June, the hottest months. West façades take the hard afternoon sun and sea glare, while north light is comparatively benign. Annual mean minimums of about 24 °C and humidity of 70–90% mean the cooling demand is driven by humidity.
- **Salt and humidity.** The site is about 0.9 km from the sea, so marine aerosols raise corrosion risk for glazing frames, fixings and plant.
- **Seismic.** Zone III (Z = 0.16) suits a stacked-floor, cantilevered structure. Wikipedia states Antilia is designed for a magnitude-8 earthquake (unverified).

## 0. Method and reference point

The footprint's bounding box is 18.96777–18.96840 N and 72.80924–72.80995 E. It is about 2,890 m² and measures about 60 × 55 m.

Two other sources agree with it:
- the CTBUH Skyscraper Center map pin at 18.968189, 72.809532 (16 m away);
- Wikipedia at 18.9681, 72.8095 (9 m away).

> **Correction to the brief.** The working coordinate **18.9680 N, 72.8104 E is not Antilia.** It lies 87 m east of Antilia's centroid and only **19 m from the CTBUH pin of Lodha Altamount** (18.968170, 72.810432), the 195 m tower next door. Wikipedia gives Lodha Altamount as 18.968314, 72.810419. **Put the model origin on 18.96806 N, 72.80958 E.**

**How the numbers were produced**
- **Distances:** haversine with R = 6,371,008.8 m, measured from the reference point above.
- **Bearings:** initial great-circle bearing from Antilia, with 0° = north.
- **Coordinate sources, in order of preference:**
  1. CTBUH Skyscraper Center map pin (`skyscrapercenter.com/map/building/{id}`);
  2. SkyscraperPage map pin;
  3. Wikipedia coordinates;
  4. OSM footprint centroid (Nominatim/Overpass; © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).

  Where two sources agree, both are named.
- **Height definitions:** CTBUH heights follow CTBUH rules, measured from the lowest significant open-air pedestrian entrance. They are not heights above sea level, which matters because Antilia sits on Cumballa Hill.
- **Naming:** in 2026 the Skyscraper Center footer reads "© 2026 Council on Vertical Urbanism" (CVU). This file still calls it "CTBUH" because that is the name Wikipedia and other sources use.

## 2. Precedent and comparison notes for the critique

### (a) Kanchanjunga Apartments: Charles Correa, 1970–83

**Location relative to Antilia**
- Address: 72 Peddar Road (Gopalrao Deshmukh Marg), Cumballa Hill, Mumbai 400026, per CTBUH and the MIT DOME record.
- **Pin: 18.965401 N, 72.806999 E** (CTBUH map). This sits on a square OSM footprint (`way 353422565`, 21.0 × 22.5 m, about 469 m²). That matches Correa's "square in plan: 21 meters x 21 meters". SquareYards' JSON-LD (18.965363, 72.806982) agrees to within 4 m.
- The building is **401 m from Antilia at a bearing of about 223° (south-west)**. It stands on the west, sea-facing side of Peddar Road, about 44 m from the road centreline, near the Kemps Corner end. Antilia lies about 0.4 km to the north-east. Its centroid is about 40 m from the Altamount Road centreline and about 110 m east of Peddar Road (OSM geometry).
- OSM tags this footprint `building:levels=10`. That is wrong and should not be used.

**Key data**

**Design logic** (quoted from the Correa/Khan text on Architexturez):
- **Climate versus views:** "the east-west axis affords the best views (of the Arabian sea to the west and the harbour to the east) and catches all the sea breezes, but also brings into the buildings the hot afternoon sun and the hard monsoon rains."
- **Verandah turned into a garden:** "to turn the verandah or buffer zone into a garden which not only protects the living areas from the sun and rain but actually thrives on them". Correa "settled upon a configuration of interlocking units which faced east and west."
- **Interlocking split levels:** "The basic interlock is that of a three and four bedroom apartment with the larger flats formed by the addition of another half level." "In section, there is a continuous variation of internal spaces best expressed as shear walls on the north and south elevations."
- **Double-height garden terraces:** "Each of the flats have large usable garden-terraces"; "6.3m cantilevered open terraces". The MIT DOME photo record describes the "two-story terraces" (Peter Serenyi, 1985).
- **RC core and slip-form:** "The central core is composed of lifts and provides the main structural element for resisting lateral loads. The central core was constructed ahead of the main structure by slip method of construction. This technique was used for the first time in India for a multistory building."
  - The same text also says "32 story reinforced concrete structure". That contradicts its own "28 storeys" and is probably a slip for the 32 apartments.

**Critique hooks** (my analysis, built on the sourced numbers above)
- **Same idea, different scale.** Both buildings place a "garden in the sky" at their centre. Perkins+Will (Architectural Record, 2007) described Antilia's "band of vertical and horizontal gardens" and a mid-height garden level drawn from Vaastu. Correa instead gives every household its own two-storey garden terrace, so the climate buffer belongs to each unit.
- **Density.** Kanchanjunga puts 32 households into about 12,000 m² of tower GFA. Antilia's "over 37,000 m²" (Wikipedia) is about 3.1 times that, for one family.

### (b) The Imperial, Tardeo: a slum-rehabilitation (SRA) project

**Origin**

The architect describes the project this way: "The MP Mill redevelopment forms part of the Government of Maharashtra's initiative to address Mumbai's critical housing shortage through the Slum Rehabilitation Scheme (SRS). The project enables the rehabilitation of approximately 2,500 slum families on a 12.6-acre site in central Mumbai by providing free, high-quality housing funded through a market-driven free-sale component." The towers "rise alongside the rehabilitation housing". Source: hafeezcontractor.com.

Project data from the same page:
- client S D Corp;
- tower site 3 acres;
- built-up area 15,00,000 sq ft;
- "Building Height 256 mtr";
- structure by JW Consultants LLP.

The main contractor's scope reads "Construction of Imperial Towers, 60 stories each including 9 Podium levels. 14 Rehabilitation Buildings", with completion in June 2009 (client S D Corporation Pvt Ltd). Source: shapoorji.in.

**How the mechanism works** (The Globe and Mail, Anuj Chopra, 31 Jan 2011)
- "Shanty towns are being flattened and their residents moved to high-rise buildings that occupy a small portion of the slum site, freeing up the rest of the land for high-end residential properties."
- The project involves "moving 2,700 slum-dwelling families from single-room huts to tiny apartments in 11 nearby buildings."
- Developers profit because "planning codes for the slum rehab projects have been relaxed … developers are allowed to exceed the normal density rules and build extra-tall buildings."
- "Since Imperial Towers opened in March, 80 per cent of its 228 units have been sold."
- An SD Corp vice-president admitted: "Some express apprehensions about getting slum neighbours."

**Criticism** (same Globe and Mail article)
- Sundar Burra, adviser to SPARC (Society for the Promotion of Area Resource Centres): "the developer is the biggest gainer – laughing his way to the bank."
- "At Imperial Towers, their units measure only 225 square feet, less than half the area of private pools for the luxury suites."
- "The 11 buildings for the slum tenants have inadequate water facilities and are separated by garbage-choked alleyways riddled with rat burrows."
- Burra again: "It's like moving people from horizontal slums to vertical slums."
- Tenants live rent-free but "must pay for electricity and maintenance … which causes many to regret the move." A resident is also quoted positively: "now I own a concrete house."

**Claim to treat with caution:** Wikipedia says the Imperial was "the first implementation of this model on a large scale". That sentence has **no citation** in the Wikipedia article.
